# Faurecia Clean Mobility – Constitutive Modeling of Stainless Steels (2017–2018)

This section presents my contribution to an industrial R&D project conducted within **Faurecia Clean Mobility (France)**, focused on the development of thermomechanical constitutive models for stainless steels used in automotive exhaust systems.

The objective of the project was to deliver validated, ready-to-use material behavior laws in the form of **material cards (“carte matériau”)** for numerical simulation in industrial design workflows.

These models were intended to support accurate prediction of material response under severe thermomechanical loading conditions representative of real service environments.

---

## Project Objective

The main goal of this work was to develop, calibrate, and validate constitutive laws describing the thermomechanical behavior of stainless steels, enabling their direct integration into finite element simulation tools used in industrial design.

---

## Methodology

The modeling workflow was structured into three main phases for each material:

---

### Phase 1 – Material Characterization

This phase focused on defining and executing experimental test plans for material characterization in laboratory conditions.

Key activities included:

* Definition of mechanical testing protocols
* Execution of experimental campaigns in laboratory environments
* Data acquisition and processing of mechanical test results
* Organization of results into structured datasets for model calibration

---

### Phase 2 – Parameter Identification & Model Calibration

This phase focused on identifying the parameters of constitutive laws using inverse modeling techniques.

Key activities included:

* Calibration of material model coefficients using experimental data
* Optimization of constitutive parameters via dedicated numerical tools
* Simulation of experimental tests using identified parameters
* Comparison between experimental and numerical results
* Iterative refinement of model coefficients to improve accuracy

**Software Used**

* ZeBuLoN (parameter identification and optimization)
* Abaqus (finite element simulation)

---

### Phase 3 – Model Validation

This phase ensured the robustness and predictive capability of the identified constitutive models under conditions closer to real industrial use.

Key activities included:

* Definition of validation test procedures
* Execution of independent experimental validation campaigns (including subcontracted laboratory tests)
* Numerical simulation of validation tests using calibrated models
* Comparison between experimental and simulated results under severe loading conditions
* Assessment of model reliability and predictive accuracy

---

## Engineering Contributions

* Development of thermomechanical constitutive models for stainless steels
* Experimental characterization and data processing for material modeling
* Parameter identification using inverse methods
* Finite element simulation of material behavior (Abaqus)
* Validation of predictive material models under industrial conditions
* Integration of material cards into engineering simulation workflows

---

## Software & Tools

* ZeBuLoN (material parameter identification)
* Abaqus (finite element modeling and simulation)
* Experimental mechanical testing platforms

---

## Industrial Impact

This work contributed to the development of reliable simulation inputs for automotive exhaust system design, enabling improved prediction of material behavior under thermomechanical loading conditions and supporting more efficient and robust engineering design processes in an industrial environment.
