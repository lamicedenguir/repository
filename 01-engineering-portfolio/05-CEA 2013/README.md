# CEA USI COR SURF Project (2013–2016)

### Surface Integrity, Material Behavior & Corrosion Modeling in Machining

This section presents the deliverables of the multi-annual **USI COR SURF research program** conducted in collaboration with **CEA Valduc (France)** between 2013 and 2016.

This project focused on the experimental and numerical investigation of **surface integrity induced by machining processes**, and its impact on **microstructural evolution, mechanical state, and electrochemical (corrosion) behavior** of oxygen-free high-conductivity copper (OFHC Cu-C2).

The work combined advanced machining experiments, surface characterization techniques, and multiphysics modeling to establish quantitative relationships between cutting conditions, subsurface material transformations, and functional performance.

---

## Research Objective

The main objective of this research program was to:

> Understand, quantify, and model the relationship between machining-induced surface integrity and corrosion resistance of OFHC copper.

A secondary objective was to evaluate whether simplified machining configurations (orthogonal cutting) could reliably represent the surface integrity generated by complex 3D machining processes.

---

## Project Structure (2013–2016)

### 2013 – Surface Integrity & Electrochemical Reactivity

This initial phase focused on experimental characterization of surface and subsurface modifications induced by **superfinishing turning of OFHC copper** and their impact on electrochemical behavior.

Key investigations included:

* Cutting force measurement (3 orthogonal components)
* Surface roughness characterization
* Residual stress analysis
* Microstructural surface and subsurface examination
* Electrochemical corrosion testing in NaClO₄ solution (1M)
* Corrosion potential measurements vs Ag/AgCl reference electrode

Two machining configurations were compared:

* 3D superfinishing turning
* Orthogonal cutting

---

### 2014 – Surface Integrity Modeling & Process Comparison

This phase extended the investigation toward **mechanical and microstructural modeling of machined sub-layers**.

Key contributions included:

* Experimental analysis of superfinishing machining mechanics
* Comparative study between face turning and orthogonal cutting
* Characterization of surface integrity evolution
* Electrochemical behavior analysis of machined surfaces
* Statistical correlation between process parameters and material response

---

### 2015 – Numerical Modeling & Multiphysics Correlation

This phase introduced **numerical simulation and predictive modeling approaches**.

Key contributions included:

* Experimental validation of orthogonal cutting mechanics
* Development of numerical models of surface integrity evolution
* Simulation of machining-induced microstructural changes
* Multiphysics coupling between mechanical and electrochemical behavior
* Development of a phenomenological model linking:

  * surface integrity → corrosion resistance

Experimental campaigns were conducted across multiple laboratories:

* LaBoMaP (Cluny, France)
* ICB (Dijon, France)
* ENISE (Saint-Étienne, France)

---

### 2016 – Advanced Modeling & PhD Completion

The final phase focused on **integrated predictive modeling and constitutive law development**.

Key outcomes:

* Development of a constitutive model for OFHC copper incorporating:

  * microstructural evolution
  * stress state dependency
* Predictive numerical model for machining-induced surface integrity
* High-confidence statistical correlation between:

  * cutting physics
  * surface integrity
  * corrosion resistance
* Completion of PhD-level research work
