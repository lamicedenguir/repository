# CIRP International Research Project – Cutting Edge Preparation & Tool Wear Analysis (2012)

This section presents my contribution to an international research project conducted in collaboration with the **CIRP (International Academy for Production Engineering)** as part of a multi-laboratory Robin Test campaign.

The objective of this study was to investigate the influence of cutting edge preparation methods on tool wear during face milling of hardened and stainless steels. The project was conducted simultaneously across several international laboratories under strictly controlled experimental conditions, ensuring full comparability, repeatability, and statistical reliability of the results.

This work contributed to a broader research effort aimed at understanding and modeling the relationship between tool edge preparation, cutting conditions, and wear evolution.

---

## Research Objective

The main objective of this study was to evaluate how different cutting edge preparation techniques influence tool wear mechanisms during rough face milling operations.

The investigated edge preparation methods included:

* Abrasive blasting
* Abrasive flow machining
* Brushing
* Drag finishing
* Honing
* Laser machining
* Magneto-abrasive machining
* Untreated inserts (reference condition)

The experiments were performed using standardized **SECO SEAN1203AFTN-M14 TiN-coated inserts** .

Two workpiece materials were studied:

* AISI 4140 QT (42CrMo4 quenched and tempered steel)
* AISI 304L (X2CrNi19-11 stainless steel)

---

## Methodology

The research methodology combined experimental machining tests with statistical and physical analysis:

### 1. Literature Review

A structured review of cutting edge preparation techniques and their influence on tool wear mechanisms.

### 2. Experimental Design

Definition of standardized face milling conditions, including tool geometry, workpiece materials, and machining parameters aligned across participating laboratories.

### 3. Tool Wear Characterization

Systematic measurement of tool wear using:

* Maximum flank wear (VBmax)
* Average flank wear (VBav)
* Chip morphology analysis
* Cutting force observation

### 4. Statistical and Multivariate Analysis

Application of **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)** to identify correlations between:

* Cutting forces
* Tool geometry and edge preparation
* Chip formation characteristics
* Wear evolution

This analysis enabled the development of a predictive model for flank wear behavior.
